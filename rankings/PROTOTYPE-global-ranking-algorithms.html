Which global ranking algorithm should Second Quint Rankings use?

Every contestant below has a hidden true strength, meaning how much people really prefer them. Simulated players run random-draw tournaments and Infinite Mode sessions, picking winners the way real people would: usually the stronger contestant, but not always. Each algorithm sees only the match results and tries to rebuild the true order.

The question: which algorithm recovers the true order best from the kind of play our site produces, while accounting for strength of opponents, holding back contestants with few matches, and following tastes as they change?

Rank contestants by:

Ranking accuracy: how closely the algorithm's order matches the true order (100 is perfect, 0 is no better than shuffling). Top 10 right: how many of the true top 10 it places in its own top 10. Unproven in top 10: contestants in its top 10 with fewer than 5 matches (lower is safer). Predicted matches right: before each day is played, the share of that day's matches whose winner the algorithm called correctly. This is the fairest real-world test, since it only uses information available at the time.

The algorithms

Elo
The classic chess rating. Every match moves both ratings by an amount that depends on how surprising the result was. It has no idea how confident it is, so a contestant who wins its first two matches can shoot up.
Glicko-2
Elo plus a confidence range (the ± number). The range shrinks as a contestant plays and grows again while it sits idle, and newer results count for more. Ratings update once per day.
TrueSkill
Microsoft's Xbox matchmaking rating. It uses a similar idea to Glicko-2, a rating plus a confidence range, but updates after every match. The name and method are Microsoft's; OpenSkill is an open equivalent.
Bradley–Terry
Re-fits all match history at once every day, so the order matches were played in doesn't matter and strength of opponents is used fully. Older matches fade out with a half-life (see Settings). Its ratings are shown on an Elo-like scale.
Win %
Wins divided by matches, ignoring who you beat. It's a baseline to show why strength of opponents matters.
